Your data probably isn’t ready for AI if your reports disagree, your key numbers live in spreadsheets, your team spends hours each week fixing data, or nobody can say who has access to what. None of these is unusual, and each one has a clear first fix. Here are six signs to look for.

## 1. Do your reports disagree with each other?

If finance and operations show different numbers for the same thing, meetings turn into arguments about whose figure is right. It usually means the same metric is calculated in different ways in different places.

**First fix:** agree one definition for each key number, write it down, and build it once in a shared platform so every report uses it.

## 2. Do your key numbers live in spreadsheets?

When important figures are copied between files by hand, one wrong paste can throw off a whole report, and nobody notices until later. An AI tool reading those files has no way to spot the mistake.

**First fix:** bring your main sources into one place before adding new tools.

## 3. Does your team spend hours each week collecting and fixing data?

If people download, clean and upload the same data every week, that time isn’t going into decisions. It’s also a sign the data needs a lot of fixing before anyone can trust it.

**First fix:** automate the weekly refresh. In one project, dealer stock that used to arrive in many shapes now refreshes twice a day without anyone touching it. See [the dealer inventory case study](/case-studies/dealer-inventory-google-vehicle-ads/).

## 4. Can nobody say who can see which data?

If access grew one request at a time, it’s hard to prove who sees what, which matters for audits and for any AI tool you connect.

**First fix:** set access by role, so people see only the data their role needs, and keep a record of changes.

## 5. Does nobody know where a number comes from?

If a figure in a report can’t be traced back to its source, it can’t be checked, and it’s hard to fix when it’s wrong.

**First fix:** document every table: what it holds, where it comes from and who owns it.

## 6. Did an AI pilot give confident, wrong answers?

An AI tool is only as good as the data under it. If a pilot gave answers that looked right but weren’t, the problem was most likely the data, not the model.

**First fix:** get the data the use case needs clean, joined and documented, then run the pilot again on data you trust.
